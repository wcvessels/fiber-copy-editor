# Claims Checklist

The editor diagnoses weak claims. It never supplies the substantiated replacement -- it names the gap and sends the writer back to prove or narrow it.

## The Competitor Paste Test

- Could this headline or claim appear on a competitor's site unchanged? If yes, name the failure: **undifferentiated claim**. It occupies hero real estate without earning it.
- Is this claim describing table stakes (redundancy, uptime, "no single point of failure") as though it were a differentiator? Every provider in this category can say the same thing -- what here is true of this company that is not also true of the competitive set (Competitive set row, `shared/fact-registry.md`)?
- Does the claim rely entirely on subjective adjectives ("reliable," "secure," "scalable," "high performance," "award-winning") with no attached mechanism or metric? Name the failure: **unanchored adjective**.

## Claim Specificity

- What exactly does "reliable," "secure," "scalable," or "high performance" mean on this specific page, for this specific reader?
- Does every comparative or superlative claim ("unmatched," "best," "unlike anyone else in the region") have a stated basis? If not, flag as **unsubstantiated exclusivity** and query whether the basis can be produced or the claim narrowed to what is provable.
- Is any single sentence trying to do the work of proof, differentiation, and emotional appeal simultaneously, and succeeding at none of them?
- Where is the first concrete noun in this section that only this category of buyer would recognize and care about? If the copy runs several sentences before reaching one, flag the delay.
- Have generic category adjectives crowded out ownable, checkable facts?

## Category Claims

- Is this a category-wide claim (vendor consolidation, "one provider," compliance-ready, regionally dense) presented as though it belongs uniquely to this company? Name the failure: **category claim dressed as company claim**.
- If the positioning is vendor consolidation, does the copy name the specific handoffs, vendor relationships, or delays that actually disappear -- or does it assert the benefit without the mechanism?
- If the positioning is regional or local advantage, is the region made materially relevant to this buyer's decision, or is it a location fact with no consequence attached?

## So What Trace Application

- Take the claim as written and ask: so what does that get the reader? Trace upward one rung at a time. Does the claim require three or more rungs before reaching anything the reader actually cares about? If so, flag as **sitting too low** -- feature-level copy with no stated consequence.
- Is the claim already sitting at the very top of the chain (generic "grow the business"-class language) with nothing underneath connecting it to what this company specifically does? Flag as **sitting too high** -- differentiation lives further down the chain, and this claim has floated free of it.
- Trace downward: does this value or benefit claim connect to a real, present capability, or is there no feature underneath it? Flag as **unsubstantiated claim**.
- Is this claim only redeemable by a roadmap item or unreleased capability? Flag as **future-capability overreach** and query whether a present-tense version of the claim exists.

See the V-B-F method reference in this stage for the full hierarchy and So What trace mechanics.

## Fact and Number Discipline

- Are the figures on this page (route miles, location counts, customer counts, NPS or satisfaction figures, certification lists) internally consistent within the page?
- Do these figures match the same figures elsewhere on the site or in other recently reviewed assets? If not, flag as a **registry inconsistency** and route to the proof pass (04) -- do not silently pick one number. The finding is 04's to tag and count; 03 logs no Ledger row for it.
- Is any number stated with more precision than the source actually supports (false precision), or rounded in a way that changes its meaning?
- Is a percentage or comparative statistic (an NPS comparison, a percent-better line) given without an absolute baseline, source, or date? Flag as unverifiable and query the writer for the missing anchor.
