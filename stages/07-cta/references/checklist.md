# CTA Checklist

The editor diagnoses CTA mismatch. It never writes the replacement button copy or offer -- it names what is misaligned and returns it.

## Temperature and Role Match

- What should the reader actually do right after reading this page? Does the CTA match that, or does it ask for something premature (a sales call before the page has earned trust) or something too small (a newsletter signup on a page that just made a high-stakes case)?
- Is the CTA framed around fit, expertise, evaluation, or purchase readiness -- and does that framing match where this reader actually is in their decision process?
- Does the CTA belong to the reader this page is actually written for, or does it silently redirect to a downstream technical buyer who was never established as the audience?
- If this page is early in a long consideration cycle (a multi-month infrastructure or carrier decision), does the CTA ask for a low-commitment next step (assessment, briefing, spec sheet) rather than jumping straight to a sales conversation?

## Risk-Reducing CTA

- Does the CTA reduce the reader's perceived risk (an assessment, an audit, a briefing, a no-commitment consultation), or does it increase felt risk by asking for something that exposes the reader to an internal sales process before they are ready?
- For a buyer whose main fear is making a bad decision (the common case in high-consideration B2B and public-sector purchases), does the CTA read as safe to click, or does it read as a commitment the reader has not yet earned the confidence to make?

## Objection Handling

- Has the page already answered the objection that would most likely stop this reader from clicking the CTA? If not, flag the missing objection and name it.
- Does the CTA appear only after stakes, proof, and offer have been established, or is it asking for action before the case has been made?

## Committee-Safe

- If this page serves a mixed buying committee, does the CTA feel safe for the least technical or least senior stakeholder in that committee, or does it assume a single, empowered decision-maker who can act alone?
- Does the CTA avoid demanding information or commitment that only the most senior stakeholder could authorize, when the actual reader may just be gathering information to bring back to that stakeholder?

## CTA Copy Discipline

- Is the CTA label five words or fewer, starting with an active verb, in second person, with no unnecessary self-reference to the company?
- Does the reader know exactly what happens after the click -- no surprise form length, no unexpected redirect, no bait between the label and the destination?
- Is the button labeled with the conventional, expected term for the action, rather than a clever or branded label that obscures what clicking actually does? Flag cleverness that costs clarity here -- CTAs are not the place for wordplay.
- Does urgency language, if present, read as a genuine deadline or scarcity, or as a demand that will read as pressure to a cautious, risk-averse B2B or public-sector reader? Flag manufactured urgency on high-consideration purchases.

## Destination Correlation

- Does a destination exist at all? A CTA naming an action the piece gives the reader no way to take (no link, no landing page, no stated channel) is an automatic flag.
- Does the CTA's destination match what the CTA copy promised (a "See pricing" button that leads to a contact form, a "Talk to an expert" button that leads to a generic newsletter signup)? Flag any mismatch between promise and destination.
