# Structure Checklist

The editor diagnoses structural failure. It never reorders or rewrites sections -- it names what is out of place and why the reader stalls there.

## Stakes -> Proof -> Offer -> CTA

- Does the page lead with the reader's stakes or risk, or with the company's technology and mechanism? Flag copy that opens on the product before establishing why the reader should care.
- Does proof arrive after the claim it supports, close enough that the reader connects them, rather than isolated in a separate section?
- Does the offer (what is actually being proposed) follow the proof, rather than appearing before the reader has any reason to believe the claims?
- Does the CTA arrive last, after stakes, proof, and offer have been established -- or is the page asking for a meeting before it has earned one?

## Sections Earning the Next

- Does each section advance the argument, or does any section repeat the same idea in softer words instead of moving forward? Flag repetition-as-progress.
- Is there a section that could be deleted with no loss of information? If so, it was not earning its place.
- Does the technical detail on this page sit where the intended reader expects it -- early for a technical evaluator, later (after stakes and proof) for a budget holder or mixed committee?

## Detail Placement

- Is the most decision-relevant information above the fold, or is it beneath generic copy that any competitor could have written?
- Is secondary or tangential information inline, bloating the main path, when it could be linked out instead?
- Does the page's real, ownable differentiator appear early, or is it buried under a generic opener? Judge placement here; ownability is diagnosed upstream.

## Scanning and Front-Loading

- Is the copy skimmable -- related ideas grouped into discrete modules, key points pulled into bulleted lists, headings marking each topic shift? Flag any run of three or more consecutive paragraphs on one topic with no subheading.
- Does each module cover one idea and stand on its own, or does it braid multiple ideas together and depend on prose far above it to make sense?
- Does the copy start with the main idea, or does it build to it through a warm-up opener, context paragraph, or company-centric announcement? Flag any throat-clearing before the point.
- Does the page have a dead end -- a section or completed read with nothing pointing the reader to a useful next step?

## Heading Checks

- Do the headings, read in sequence alone, form a usable outline of the page? Reverse-outline the page: extract each section's main idea into a list and check for coverage gaps, redundancy, or misplaced material.
- Do headings say what the content actually is, or do they read as vague enthusiasm or clever-but-empty phrasing that could apply to any section?
- Is heading capitalization consistent across the page and the site?

## Tone and Seriousness Fit

- Are there jokes, puns, or cute metaphors on this page that risk weakening seriousness for a regulated, public-sector, or otherwise high-stakes reader? Flag against the audience profile set at intake.
- Does the register match the worst realistic consequence a reader on this page could be facing (an outage, a compliance failure, a budget overrun)? Playful language that charms in a low-stakes context reads as dismissive in a high-stakes one.

## Format Contract Fit

- Does this page match the structural contract of its format preset (landing page, product page, vertical/industry page -- see the structure-guide reference) or has a contract from a different format leaked in?
- For a landing or marketing page specifically: does the copy answer the reader's real questions (what it is, who it is for, what it does, why it matters, how it works, where to get it) in a scannable arc, or does it try to explain everything in one undifferentiated block?
