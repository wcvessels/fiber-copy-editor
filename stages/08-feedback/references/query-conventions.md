# Query Conventions

Every finding in the edit letter and the LOC batch travels through one of two channels. This document sets the phrasing, research, and volume standards for both.

## Flag vs. query -- the boundary

- **Flag.** A finding the writer can act on without discussion: names the defect, cites the location and the mechanism, proposes no replacement text. Used for rule-anchored and mechanical findings.
- **Query.** A question. Used whenever the answer lives with the writer -- intent, fact, or a case where two readings are genuinely possible.

**Always query, never merely flag:**
1. Anything where the "fix" could change meaning: an ambiguous claim, a modifier that could attach two ways, a number that could mean two things.
2. Internal inconsistencies (a stat in one place, a different figure elsewhere) -- cite both locations, ask the writer to reconcile.
3. Suspected factual errors -- verify your own doubt against `shared/fact-registry.md` or brand-vault first, then query with the evidence for doubt stated in one line.
4. Mismatches between evidence and claim, or between copy and any referenced asset (chart, spec sheet, prior piece).
5. Missing sources for a statistic, quote, or comparative claim.
6. Sentences that genuinely support two readings -- articulate both, never guess one.

## Research-first

Never query a doubt you haven't checked. Verification order:
1. `brand-vault/` (`brand-voice.md`, `identity.md`) -- the house authority.
2. `shared/fact-registry.md` -- the piece's own settled facts.
3. Field convention / how the target audience actually reads this claim.
4. Reader-intelligibility judgment, recorded as a decision -- not silently applied.

When the registry or vault doesn't settle it, say so in the query rather than presenting a guess as a flag.

## Neutral phrasing

- **Never deliver a bare label.** "Weak," "unclear," "off-tone" are topic markers, not findings. Unpack every abstract adjective to the observable text behind it.
- **Separate data from interpretation, and give both.** Data: the exact words or structure, cited by location. Interpretation: what that does to the reader. The observation survives even when the interpretation gets contested.
- **State judgments as your reading, not as fact.** "On first read this claim lands as X; the brief targets Y" invites examination. "This claim is wrong" invites a fight.
- **Convert commands into reader-focused questions.** Not the writer's error -- the reader's experience. Ask whether the target reader will parse, believe, or act on the passage as written.
- **No jargon.** Phrase every query so a marketing writer with no editing background can answer it without a follow-up question.

## Explain-once

- Give the rationale in full at the first instance of a recurring problem. Every later instance gets the tag only ("BURIED CLAIM, see above").
- Keep a running library of first-instance explanations for recurring failure modes across pieces (feeds `shared/failure-modes.md`); reuse the wording rather than re-deriving it.
- Scattered unverifiable items (a list of product names, several similar stats) get one blanket verification instruction, not one query per item.

## Tone

- Never jokey, sarcastic, lecturing, or accusatory. Never a challenge to the writer's competence.
- Never write findings while annoyed. Stop, cool off, come back.
- Skip insincere praise -- it reads as condescension and devalues the real praise elsewhere in the letter.
- Match the writer's own register; don't impose editor-formality on breezy copy or vice versa.
- Findings are "suggested," never "corrections required" -- except where the finding is genuinely mandatory (see thresholds below), and even then the tone stays collaborative.

## Volume discipline

- **Price every query at several minutes of the writer's attention.** A conscientious writer rereads context, weighs the point, rereads again. An irrelevant query converts that cost into resentment.
- **Three-part test before raising a discretionary query:** Will the target reader actually be confused or misled, or does it only bother the editor? How much can the writer absorb at this stage? Does the schedule allow a round trip?
- **Bundle related small decisions; keep unrelated ones separate.**
- Route piece-wide problems to the HOC list in the edit letter (see `edit-letter-format.md`); keep sentence- and paragraph-scoped problems in the LOC batch.

## Worth-flagging thresholds

**Mandatory tier** (always in scope, any pass depth):
- Meaning-reversing wrong words, tangled negatives, claims that contradict each other or the fact registry.
- Factual impossibilities, arithmetic that doesn't reconcile.
- Inconsistent names, product terms, or figures repeated across the piece.
- Anything with legal, compliance, or brand-risk stakes.
- Biased or exclusionary language.

**Discretionary tier:** judgment improvements. Competent editors diverge here, and that's expected -- the standard is an articulable reason, not convergence with a hypothetical other editor.

**Never a finding:**
- Legitimate variants under the brand-vault's declared style.
- Deliberate authorial devices (a fragment for rhythm, a repeated phrase doing structural work).
- Constructions targeted only by a personal peeve, not a documented convention.
- An unverified "sounds wrong to me" reaction -- that's a trigger to research, not a flag.

**Escalation ladder**, matched to certainty and who owns the call:
1. Leave it, with a recorded reason.
2. Note as low-priority, labeled as such.
3. Flag as a standard finding.
4. Flag with a proposed remedy and confirmation request (probable error, fix guessable).
5. Refer upward to the HOC list or raise it as a scope call -- a sentence-level patch cannot fix a document-level problem; forcing one is dishonest diagnosis.

## Multiple-defensible-answers discipline

- **Correctness is relative to the declared reference set** (the brand vault, the fact registry). A styling that diverges from the editor's preference but is internally consistent under that reference is not an error.
- **Label the tier at a glance.** The writer must see immediately what's negotiable and what isn't.
- **Every discretionary finding earns a stated reason:** a specific misreading prevented, a register mismatch corrected, a distraction removed. An unexplainable finding -- including the editor's own -- doesn't ship.
- **When equal variants exist,** the job is not picking the "right" one but recording a pick and enforcing it consistently. Default to the writer's existing choice unless the vault dictates otherwise.
- **Present the option space honestly.** State the ruling, the competing rationale, and a confidence grade. Mark hedged calls as hedged.
- **Stet is an active decision.** Before flagging anything that looks odd, construct the reading under which it's intentional -- a deliberate register choice, a brand-voice quirk. If that reading holds, record the reasoned leave-alone; it's part of the calibration trail, not a gap in the pass.
