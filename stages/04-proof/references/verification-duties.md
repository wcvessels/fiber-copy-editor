# Verification Duties

The editor verifies what reference material and the document itself can settle. Everything else is flagged and routed to the writer -- never adjudicated by the editor. Unverifiable never means silently skipped; it means a documented handoff.

## What the Editor Verifies

- Spellings of well-known names, places, and product or service terms, against the workspace's own reference material. Is an unfamiliar or nonstandard-styled name present? Route it to the writer as a verification request, not a guess.
- Internal consistency: do numbers that should add up, actually add up? Do two claims on the same page contradict each other? Does date or timeline logic hold?
- Quantitative sanity: does a stated percentage change make mathematical sense (a quantity can rise more than 100 percent but cannot fall more than 100 percent; a stated "increase" and "percentage points" are not interchangeable)? Is precision implied that the source cannot support?
- Correlation: does every claim match its supporting chart or stat block? Does every CTA go where the copy says it goes? Do headings match navigation and cross-references?
- Usability details: does the phone number, address, or link actually work from where the reader sits?

## What Gets Flagged as the Writer's Problem

- Factual claims, statistics, product specifications, performance numbers, competitive comparisons, and customer results are the writer's responsibility to substantiate. The editor's job is to confirm that substantiation exists and is current -- not to independently fact-check every figure from scratch.
- Does this page make a claim whose truth the editor cannot verify against workspace reference material? Flag it and query the source. Do not silently pass it, and do not silently reject it.
- Is there a claim that may carry legal exposure -- comparative language about a named competitor, a testimonial or identifiable customer reference without visible consent, a compliance claim that may need substantiation? Flag and route to the writer. The editor is the early-warning system here, never the judge.
- Is there quoted, charted, or excerpted material that is not the writer's own? Flag for a permissions check before publication.

## Stale-Registry-Entry Handling

The workspace's network, scale, and company-fact figures (route miles, on-net or lit location counts, data center counts, customer counts, satisfaction scores) function as a registry that multiple pages draw from. This registry drifts -- pages get updated at different times, and old figures survive in footers and older assets after hero stats move on.

- Does this page's figure match the same figure elsewhere in the current review set? If not, this is a **registry inconsistency**, not a style issue -- it is a credibility issue with buyers who verify claims before signing.
- When two figures conflict, does the editor pick the "right" one? No. Query: cite both locations, state the discrepancy plainly, and ask the writer to reconcile against a single source of truth. Never assert which figure is correct without an authoritative reference to point to.
- Is a figure on this page older or rounder than a figure seen elsewhere in the workspace, suggesting it has not been updated since a newer stat was published? Flag as **possibly stale** and query rather than silently updating it to match the newer figure.
- Where the workspace has an established fact sheet or single source of truth, does this page's figure match it? If no such source exists yet, flag the absence itself as a finding -- the registry inconsistency is a symptom, and the missing single source is the cause.

## Boundary

- Does the problem live in the words, or somewhere else -- a compliance question only counsel can answer, a stat that depends on an internal system the editor cannot query, a design decision that misleads regardless of copy? Route these to their owning function. A copy finding cannot fix a fact the editor has no way to check.
- Has verification scope been agreed at intake? If not, flag the gap: what the editor verifies versus what it only flags should be explicit before the pass begins, not discovered mid-review.
