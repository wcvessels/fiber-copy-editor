# Audience Model

Every piece is profiled fresh on the dimensions below, not slotted into a fixed persona. The presets are starting points, not a closed list; a writer-defined profile is fine when none fits.

## The Four Dimensions

### 1. Temperature

Cold general public -> aware prospect -> active evaluator -> committee signer.

- Cold-reach copy: near-zero jargon budget; stakes must land before proof.
- Aware / evaluating readers: room for proof and differentiation once stakes are established.
- Signers: read for risk, rarely for detail; copy must arm the champion, not persuade the signer directly.

### 2. Technical Depth

Layperson -> operator -> specialist.

Drives the jargon rule below: specialist audiences need dense technical vocabulary, and simplifying it destroys credibility. Layperson and budget audiences need everything translated to risk, cost, and continuity.

### 3. Decision Role

Every piece aimed at a group purchase profiles two, sometimes three, roles:

- **Champion** -- researches, reads, pushes the deal internally. This is the register and altitude target: write to the champion.
- **Signer** -- approves, may never read the piece. This sets the risk lens: the proof and framing the champion needs in hand to defend the piece upward.
- **Influencer** -- shapes the decision without approving or reading in depth (e.g., a technical reviewer). Profile only when the piece has a distinct influencer audience.

Cold pieces have neither champion nor signer. Judge them on earning the next click, not on committee dynamics.

**The champion test:** copy passes when the champion could defend it, line by line, to the signer. If the champion would have to translate or apologize for a line before showing it upward, the line fails.

### 4. Risk Lens

What the signer (or, for cold pieces, no one) stands to lose:

- Career risk -- a bad vendor call reflects on the champion
- Compliance exposure -- a missed cert or audit failure
- Budget defensibility -- can this spend be justified upward
- Curiosity only -- cold public, no risk lens exists yet

## The Presets

| Preset | Temperature | Technical depth | Champion / signer | Risk lens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IT director (enterprise / DIA) | Aware to evaluating | Operator | IT director champions; budget holder or CFO signs | Career risk, budget defensibility |
| Carrier / wholesale (dark fiber) | Evaluating | Specialist | Network engineer champions and often signs | Technical credibility, route and control risk |
| Government purchasing officer | Evaluating to committee | Operator to layperson | Purchasing officer champions; agency leadership signs | Compliance exposure, budget defensibility, auditability |
| Non-technical budget holder (regulated verticals) | Aware to evaluating | Layperson | Department head champions; finance or compliance signs | Compliance exposure, budget defensibility |
| Cold general public | Cold | Layperson | None | Curiosity only |
| Trade press / journalist * | Non-buyer (off-funnel) | Operator to layperson | None -- conduit to buyers | Story value, own credibility |
| Industry analyst * | Non-buyer (off-funnel) | Specialist | Influencer; no signer | Analytical independence |
| Existing customer (renewal / upsell) * | Retained -- warm, or skeptical after a service failure | Operator; budget holder re-enters at renewal | Original champion if still in seat; otherwise treat as re-acquisition | Switching cost vs. re-justifying the decision; inverts when at-risk |

\* Seeded at build, validated by review, not yet by a piece run through the pipeline -- revise on first use.

Seeded-row notes:

- An at-risk renewal reader needs the grievance acknowledged before any upsell.
- Champion departure is a primary churn path -- profile the actual current decision-maker, not the original champion by default.
- Analysts read simplification as spin -- full technical density holds.

## Jargon Calibration Rules

Jargon is not inherently bad. It is either credibility or a wall, and which one it is depends entirely on the reader.

- **Specialist audiences** (e.g., carrier/wholesale): precise technical vocabulary signals competence. Vague, simplified language reads as amateur. Do not flag correct technical terms here as jargon.
- **Layperson and budget audiences**: every acronym or technical term must be translated to risk, cost, or outcome in the same sentence or the one immediately following. An untranslated acronym is a wall.
- **Legitimacy test** for any flagged term: (1) will this audience universally understand it, and (2) does it point to one specific referent for this audience. A term that passes both is not jargon for this reader, even if it fails for another.
- **Default posture**: when in doubt, translate. Over-translating for a specialist is a minor register mismatch; under-translating for a layperson loses the reader entirely.

## Altitude Calibration

Altitude is how broad-and-lofty versus technical-and-specific a line reads.

- **Too high**: a vague outcome any competitor could claim, disconnected from what the product actually does.
- **Too low**: feature and mechanism talk with no reason for this reader to care.
- **Calibration rule**: the more technical the reader, the lower the altitude can go, provided value is still stated. The less the reader knows about the solution category, the broader the message must stay to orient them first.
- Meet the reader at their current working reality, not at several-years-ahead expertise.

## Curse-of-Knowledge Check

Writers close to the product unconsciously assume the reader shares their context. When copy presumes knowledge the profiled reader would not have -- a term never defined, a leap in reasoning skipped -- name the failure as curse-of-knowledge and state the specific assumed knowledge. Expect this failure by default in copy written by subject-matter experts; check for it every time, not only when something looks off.

## Single-Reader Check

Copy is read by one person at a time, not by "businesses" in the abstract. Copy addressed to a corporate entity, or that tries to serve every segment simultaneously, has no defined reader -- the specific wants that make copy land have been averaged out. Flag as no-defined-reader.

When a piece must genuinely straddle two audiences, the shared need should carry most of the message and the divergent need should be confined to a single, clearly marked section. A message diluted evenly across audiences is a strategy failure, not a tone problem.

## Champion Check

Restate before every 02-audience-fit pass: who is the champion, who is the signer, and does every claim requiring proof give the champion something to point to. A piece pitched at the signer's altitude when the champion is the actual reader is mistargeted -- flag as register-mismatch, decision-role.
